# What we learned from OCR project presentations

The user requested a review of Haoran Wei's OCR open-source presentation style. The references below are their official repositories; this is an editorial reading of the presentation, not a claim about what caused their popularity.

| Reference | Memorable proposition | Visible entry points |
|---|---|---|
| [GOT-OCR2.0](https://github.com/Ucas-HaoranWei/GOT-OCR2.0) | General OCR Theory / a unified end-to-end OCR model | Paper, model, demos, inference and evaluation |
| [DeepSeek-OCR](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-OCR) | Contexts Optical Compression | Paper, weights, runnable inference, visual token configurations and visualization |
| [DeepSeek-OCR 2](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-OCR-2) | Visual Causal Flow / human-like visual encoding | Paper, models and inference |

Our interpretation: state one research question people can remember, show the input-to-output transformation immediately, connect the visual proof to measurable evidence, and make the first runnable example easy to find. Then provide enough source evidence for others to inspect and extend the work.

WireSight applies this structure with **A Visual Interface for 3D Annotation** and **See the scene. Label the points.** A real interactive 3D result leads the page; the film then shows top-down height evidence, exact recorded visual paths, complementary observations and original-point execution. The page connects directly to pinned data, a small real replay and complete-scope metric recomputation.

The larger question is how an existing visual observer can interact with very large 3D inputs through task-driven observations. Wires are the demonstrated case. Our prototype has no measured visual-token compression ratio or controlled superiority comparison, and no held-out generalization claim. The OCR projects' compression results, model designs and popularity are not evidence for this prototype's performance.
